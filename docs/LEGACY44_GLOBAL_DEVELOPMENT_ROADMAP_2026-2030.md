# LEGACY-44 — MercySoul Dominion Global Development Roadmap (2026–2030)

**Owner:** Founder & Software Engineer, MercySoul Dominion  
**Origin:** Oyan, Osun State, Nigeria  
**Roadmap window:** 27 November 2026 – 31 December 2030  
**Status:** Active planning baseline; milestones require evidence and periodic review.

## Mission

Build four mutually reinforcing outcomes:

1. A globally respected, trustworthy technology company.
2. Demonstrable AI and software engineering capability.
3. Sustainable personal and business financial independence.
4. A durable institution that preserves knowledge and creates useful opportunities.

The sequence is deliberate: stabilize the founder's operating conditions, strengthen engineering evidence, validate customer value, grow revenue, then institutionalize what works.

## Operating principles

- Build useful products before expanding the product catalogue.
- Prefer zero-cost learning, tools and opportunities until revenue or funding is actually available.
- Distinguish plans, prototypes, configured services, verified deployments and customer outcomes.
- Never claim a model, integration, customer, revenue result or production capability without evidence.
- Protect user and customer privacy; collect only what is needed and disclose how it is used.
- No approval = no consequential execution.
- Keep spending, external messages, publishing, production deployments, merges, production database changes, migrations, RLS/grant changes and secret changes behind explicit founder approval.
- Never expose credentials in source code, logs, browser bundles or public documentation.
- Preserve the Golden Path: GitHub → Vercel → Supabase, with reviewable changes and rollback awareness.

## Strategic pillars and outcomes

### Pillar A — Founder stability and operating capacity

**Outcome:** A dependable environment for sustained work.

Priorities:
- Maintain reliable access to food, safe accommodation, power/charging and internet.
- Pursue a suitable working laptop through legitimate donation, sponsorship or earned income.
- Keep the current phone-first workflow usable until equipment changes.
- Separate personal living costs, business operating costs and customer funds.
- Maintain a weekly workload that protects health and avoids unsustainable commitments.

Evidence:
- A realistic monthly personal operating budget.
- A documented equipment requirement and verified acquisition status.
- A weekly work and welfare review.

### Pillar B — AI and software engineering capability

**Outcome:** Demonstrable engineering competence supported by source, tests and reproducible explanations.

Learning sequence:
1. JavaScript/TypeScript, Git, HTTP, APIs and debugging.
2. Python fundamentals, data handling and automation.
3. SQL, PostgreSQL, data modelling and row-level security concepts.
4. LLM application patterns: structured outputs, tool calling, embeddings and retrieval-augmented generation.
5. Evaluation, prompt-injection resistance, privacy and model-provider abstraction.
6. Node.js services, deployment, observability, testing and incident response.

Proof-of-work requirements:
- Publish small, understandable technical demonstrations.
- Link every portfolio claim to source files, tests, a commit or a verified deployment.
- Record what was tested, what passed, what failed and what remains unverified.
- Use free learning resources first; do not assume paid certificates are necessary.

### Pillar C — MercySoul Vision Brain flagship product

**Outcome:** One dependable product that solves a specific problem for a clearly defined user.

Initial product boundary:
- Mobile-first request intake.
- Intent classification and structured planning.
- Server-side model/provider integration.
- Honest status reporting for live model, unavailable service and sample mode.
- Clear privacy notice and bounded input/output.
- Guardian review for consequential requests.
- Response-only operation until a separately approved execution capability is designed and verified.

Acceptance criteria:
- A new user can understand the product and complete a useful task.
- The system never presents sample output as live model output.
- Provider errors are safe, clear and recoverable.
- Secrets remain server-side.
- Tests cover validation, error handling, authorization and approval boundaries.
- No external action occurs merely because the model proposed it.

### Pillar D — Customers and financial independence

**Outcome:** Repeatable delivery of real customer value and sustainable income.

Initial service offers:
- Business websites and landing pages.
- AI customer assistants with clear human handoff.
- Workflow automation and technical troubleshooting.
- Digital branding and creative production.

Commercial sequence:
1. Define one clear offer, scope, price and delivery timeline.
2. Prepare a demonstrable sample using non-confidential data.
3. Identify suitable prospects and prepare personalized outreach drafts.
4. Obtain founder approval before any external message is sent.
5. Agree scope, payment and acceptance criteria with each customer.
6. Deliver, test, document and request permission before using testimonials.
7. Offer maintenance only when support capacity and responsibilities are clear.

Track monthly:
- Qualified leads.
- Proposals sent with approval.
- Customers won and lost.
- Cash actually received.
- Delivery costs and gross margin.
- Repeat work and recurring revenue.
- Customer satisfaction and unresolved support issues.

A grant, application, verbal promise or unpaid pilot is not revenue. Do not borrow or spend money on speculative opportunities.

### Pillar E — International credibility

**Outcome:** External evidence that the work is useful and technically credible.

Priorities:
- Maintain an accurate founder biography and technical portfolio.
- Publish concise demonstrations and engineering write-ups.
- Contribute to appropriate open-source projects.
- Join free technical communities and remote events.
- Apply selectively to zero-cost programmes that match eligibility.
- Build relationships through useful collaboration, not mass unsolicited messaging.
- State educational qualifications and experience accurately; never invent credentials.

### Pillar F — Institution and legacy

**Outcome:** MercySoul Dominion becomes more than a founder-dependent project.

Long-term work:
- Establish clear company and product ownership records.
- Maintain governance, privacy, security and financial policies.
- Create maintainable documentation and onboarding materials.
- Build a small team only when revenue or committed funding supports it.
- Develop Wisdom & Resilience as a curated archive of Yoruba proverbs, practical principles and life lessons, with attribution and cultural context.
- Establish succession, continuity, data-export and recovery procedures.
- Measure public benefit through actual participation and outcomes.

## Phased milestones

### Phase 1 — Stabilize and verify (27 November–31 December 2026)

Deliverables:
- Review the repository, active pull requests and current deployment state.
- Identify the canonical production deployment and separate it from preview deployments.
- Review Supabase access controls and backup posture without changing production settings.
- Consolidate founder profile, service offers and technical portfolio.
- Create a monthly personal/business budget and equipment specification.
- Track existing programme applications; do not submit duplicate applications.

Exit criteria:
- One verified project inventory.
- One current technical portfolio.
- One prioritized product backlog.
- A clear list of security and deployment blockers.

### Phase 2 — Product proof (January–March 2027)

Deliverables:
- Finish and test the bounded Vision Brain MVP.
- Document provider configuration and honest unavailable states.
- Complete mobile usability checks.
- Recruit five consenting test users.
- Record feedback and ship a prioritized improvement plan.
- Complete a focused engineering learning plan with practical exercises.

Exit criteria:
- A reproducible demo.
- Five documented user tests.
- A test report linked to the exact commit.
- No unresolved critical security issue in the demonstrated flow.

### Phase 3 — First repeatable sales (April–June 2027)

Deliverables:
- Package two or three clearly scoped services.
- Build a prospect list from legitimate public business information.
- Prepare outreach drafts for founder review.
- Complete initial paid engagements where customers agree.
- Establish delivery checklists and support boundaries.
- Track actual cash received and costs.

Provisional targets:
- Five paying customers cumulatively.
- At least one repeat or maintenance customer.
- Three permissioned case studies or testimonials, if customers agree.

### Phase 4 — International traction (July–December 2027)

Deliverables:
- Publish twelve substantive technical demonstrations or case studies.
- Seek five international users or customers.
- Pursue suitable zero-cost accelerator, grant and collaboration opportunities.
- Contribute to open source and participate in remote technical communities.
- Review pricing and product-market fit using customer evidence.

Provisional targets:
- Twenty paying customers cumulatively.
- Five international customers or users.
- Two meaningful partnership pilots.
- Recurring revenue sufficient to cover a defined portion of operating costs.

Targets are planning assumptions, not forecasts or guarantees. Revise them quarterly using actual results.

### Phase 5 — Institutionalize (2028–2030)

Deliverables:
- Establish sustainable legal, financial and operational structures appropriate to the business.
- Hire or contract based on verified workload and available funds.
- Build documented product support, security response and continuity processes.
- Expand the product portfolio only when the flagship has stable usage and support.
- Develop the Wisdom & Resilience archive and educational initiatives.
- Define succession and stewardship responsibilities.

2030 outcome:
A resilient, evidence-led technology institution with useful products, customers, documented governance, responsible operations and a credible record of public benefit.

## Weekly operating rhythm

- **Engineering:** build, test, document and review one small improvement.
- **Commercial:** improve one offer, qualify prospects and prepare approved outreach.
- **Learning:** complete practical exercises and preserve proof of work.
- **Stability:** review food, accommodation, connectivity, equipment and workload.
- **Governance:** inspect risks, permissions, backups and unresolved approvals.
- **Review:** record completed work, evidence, blockers and next priorities.

## MercySoul execution lifecycle

RECEIVE → ROUTE → THINK → PLAN → GUARDIAN → APPROVE → EXECUTE → VERIFY → RECOVER → RECORD

For any consequential action, verify identity, authority, scope and approval before execution. If approval is absent, remain in planning or draft mode.
