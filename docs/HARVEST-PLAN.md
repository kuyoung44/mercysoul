### Soul Intelligence price moderation standard

**UNDERSTAND → SCOPE → ESTIMATE → EXPLAIN → CUSTOMER APPROVES → DELIVER → VERIFY.**

- Price in proportion to verified requirements and actual delivery effort; do not upsell features the customer does not need.
- Show starting prices clearly; provide a written quote when requirements change the cost.
- Disclose exclusions such as hosting, domains, taxes, paid API usage, extra revisions, and ongoing maintenance.
- Do not promise revenue, business growth, perfect reliability, or features that have not been tested.
- Do not begin chargeable work until the customer approves the written scope and quote.
- Treat these prices as proposed starting points; review after real enquiries, delivery costs, customer feedback, and sustainable margins are measured.

## Customer-to-delivery workflow

1. **Discover:** Explain the offer and show a real example or working demo.
2. **Enquire:** Collect only necessary customer and project information.
3. **Qualify:** Confirm requirements, feasibility, timeline, and total price.
4. **Quote:** Record the agreed scope, exclusions, delivery date, and revision policy.
5. **Approve:** Obtain customer approval of the quote before starting chargeable work.
6. **Pay:** Use an authorized payment provider and verify payment server-side. Never trust a screenshot or browser-only status.
7. **Build:** Deliver the agreed work with least-privilege access and protected credentials.
8. **Test:** Check the output against written acceptance criteria on mobile and desktop where relevant.
9. **Deliver:** Share the verified deliverable, usage notes, and support route.
10. **Retain:** Request honest feedback and offer follow-on support only where it provides genuine value.

## First 7-day execution sequence

### Day 1 — Verify products
- Confirm the production commit, deployment state, domain routing, and live output for Vision Brain and Build Flash.
- Run the actual customer workflow, not just a homepage availability check.
- Record failures, missing environment configuration, and known limitations.
- Do not change billing or weaken security to bypass a deployment limit.

### Day 2 — Package offers
- Publish clear scope, starting prices, delivery time, revisions, exclusions, cancellation/refund handling, and support.
- Make the enquiry and order route easy to use on mobile.

### Day 3 — Demonstrate
- Produce one honest, working demonstration for each offer.
- Label prototypes clearly and distinguish demos from production functionality.
- Include a direct contact route.

### Day 4 — Order records and payment safety
- Create an order record with a unique reference, scope, amount, status, timestamps, and delivery state.
- Keep payment secrets server-side.
- Verify provider webhooks/signatures and idempotency before marking an order paid.
- Do not enable live payments until the provider account, settlement path, and required business checks are verified.

### Day 5 — Customer acquisition
- Share focused demos with relevant Nigerian businesses, creators, and entrepreneurs.
- Use direct outreach and genuine communities before spending materially on ads.
- Track enquiries, qualified leads, quotes, conversions, and customer questions.

### Day 6 — Deliver and measure
- Complete real customer work to the agreed scope.
- Record delivery time, direct costs, defects, revisions, and customer feedback.
- Do not publish testimonials without permission.

### Day 7 — Review
- Report enquiries, paid orders, completed work, gross revenue, operating costs, and unresolved issues.
- Improve the offer with evidence of customer needs.
- Do not describe scenarios or targets as actual earnings.

## Weekly scorecard

Track qualified enquiries, quotes sent, verified paid orders, completed deliveries, on-time delivery, refunds/rework, gross revenue, direct delivery costs, repeat customers/referrals, and production incidents.

## Technical and governance gates

- Keep deployments on Vercel; do not migrate to Render.
- Never expose API keys, service-role keys, or payment credentials in frontend code or Git.
- Require authorization for production changes and consequential external actions.
- Preserve human review for production publication, refunds, sensitive data, and other consequential decisions.
- Verify the exact deployed commit and re-fetch the public page before reporting a release as live.
- If a platform quota blocks deployment, record the blocker and retry only after the limit resets; do not change the plan or billing without authorization.
